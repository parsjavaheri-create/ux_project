# Step 3 · Three insights

Affinity map: img/affinity-map.png

## Insight 1
We saw groups spend 12-15 minutes scrolling streaming apps and still give up or rewatch an old movie; nobody wants to be the one who picks a bad movie for the group, leading to endless vetoes (I'm fine with whatever)
So the design must keep swipes private until a shared match happens.

## Insight 2
We saw users hesitate to swipe right on a movie poster alone and immediately look for the trailer or runtime before deciding
because a poster and title do not tell them whether the movie fits the group's current mood or how late they will be up
so the design must display runtime and genre directly on the swipe card alongside a manual (Watch trailer) button that plays on tap without jarring autoplay

## Insight 3
We saw friend groups abandon movie-picking tools when everyone had to download an app, create an account, or filter out unavailable titles manually.
because movie night is a spontaneous, low-patience moment where extra setup kills the mood before anyone even starts swiping
so the design must allow quick room joining (up to 4 friends) with upfront streaming-service filters and an immediate top-liked recommendation once a round finishes.

## My hypothesis from step 1
Right about: Friend groups struggle with decision fatigue when choosing a movie together and prefer fast, familiar swipe gestures over debating lists out loud.
Wrong about: Assuming users only needed a poster and short synopsis to swipe—participants consistently wanted runtime and an on-demand trailer before committing to a like.
